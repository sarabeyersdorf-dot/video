Agent Venture Fund

You fund a pool. Each AI agent gets a small budget to run one business. Every month the agents that lose money are shut down, their leftover cash goes back to the pool, and the profitable agents get more. Run the simulation below to see how the money behaves before you put real dollars in.

Rule 1 · Seed

Every agent starts with the same budget

Each agent gets a fixed seed to cover AI usage, software and ads. The rest of your money sits in a reserve pool.

Rule 2 · Grace

New agents get five months to find customers

No one is judged in the first five months, since search traffic and shop sales take time to build. After that, they're reviewed monthly.

Rule 3 · Shut down

Lose money over the review window and you're out

An agent whose profit over the last few months is zero or negative, or whose cash runs out, is shut down. Its remaining cash returns to the pool.

Rule 4 · Reward

Profitable agents get growth money

Each month, agents that are past their grace period and making money can draw growth money from the pool, up to what they earned. Optionally, a new agent copies the best performer's playbook.

Rule 5 · Pay you

Profit above a cushion comes back to you

Once an agent holds three months of costs, part of anything beyond that is swept to your account.

How to actually run it

Rollout plan

Weeks 1–2

Set up the container

  • Form one LLC that owns every agent business. You stay the legal owner; an AI can't sign contracts or hold accounts.
  • Open a separate business bank account. Issue each agent a virtual card with a hard monthly limit equal to its budget.
  • One shared ledger (a spreadsheet or database) where every dollar in and out is logged per agent.
Months 1–3

Pilot small

  • Start with $5,000: 3 agents at $1,200 each and $1,400 in reserve.
  • Each agent runs on Claude with tools for a website, payments, email and ads, and writes a weekly report.
  • You approve any single spend over $100 and anything customer-facing at first.
Month 4 onward

Turn on the rules

  • The monthly review runs automatically from the ledger: shut down, reallocate, sweep profit to you.
  • Winners' playbooks become the starting instructions for new agents.
  • Add money only after at least one agent has been profitable for three straight months.

Guardrails before any real money moves

  • Expect most agents to fail. In the default run, most agents are shut down. The model only pays off when one or two winners grow. Only fund what you can afford to lose entirely.
  • No real estate. Agents may not offer real estate services, referrals, leads or advice, and nothing they publish may use your name, Legacy Properties or your DRE numbers. This keeps their work separate from your license.
  • Hard spending caps, enforced by the bank. Rely on card limits, not on the agent following instructions.
  • Talk to your CPA about how the LLC reports agent income and losses, and to an attorney about terms of service and liability for each business.
